Open with your strongest, plainest sentence. The first paragraph gets a drop cap automatically, so make it count. Keep paragraphs short and let one idea breathe before moving to the next.

Write the way you teach: evidence first, jargon last. Where a claim leans on research, name it — readers trust a writer who shows the receipts. You can link out like this when it helps.

Use subheads to give the piece a spine

A subhead every few hundred words keeps a reader oriented and makes the article skimmable. Use bold sparingly, for the one phrase in a paragraph that carries the point.

Pull a single sentence you want to land into a blockquote. Used once, it gives the essay a beat. Used five times, it’s noise.

Lists work when the items are genuinely parallel:

  • One clear idea per line.
  • Keep them short — a list is a summary, not a paragraph in disguise.
  • Three to five items reads best.

Close by returning to the opening idea and giving the reader one thing to take with them. End on a sentence, not a shrug.
