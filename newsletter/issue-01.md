## Layout

A short letter from Jordan, not a designed marketing email. One column, mostly text, no hero image. That fits the Essayist look of The Brief, and plain emails are more likely to land in Gmail's Primary tab than image-heavy ones.

1. **Opening note** (first issue only): what this is, what to expect, why you write it. About 120 words.
2. **This month's essay**: title, a 2 to 3 sentence teaser, one button to read it on the site. The teaser carries the essay's opening observation, not its conclusion, so there's a reason to click.
3. **One question back**: ask readers to reply. Replies tell inbox providers people want your mail, and they give you topics.
4. **Sign-off**: the tagline line, your name, credentials.
5. **Footer**: the same educational disclaimer the site uses. Kit adds the unsubscribe link and mailing address automatically.

From issue 2 on, drop the opening note and the format becomes: one line of intro, the essay, the question back. Same shape every month.

**Styling in Kit:** use a plain text template (Kit's "Classic" text style). Georgia for headings (email clients can't load Newsreader), navy `#1B2A4A` button with white text, and at most one thin gold `#B5823F` rule under the masthead. No logo image needed.

Issue No. 1 · [[Month]] 2026

Welcome to the first issue, and thank you for signing up before there was anything to read.

Here is what to expect. Once a month I'll send one essay on investing, planning, or the decisions that connect them. Each one starts with something I've seen, in the practice, in the classroom, or in the markets, and ends with something you can use. It should take about as long to read as a cup of coffee.

Why write it? Most financial advice is sold, not taught. I spend my weeks doing both: advising families on their biggest financial decisions and teaching economics and retirement planning at the university level. The practice keeps the teaching real. The teaching keeps the advice clear. This newsletter is the teaching half, open to anyone who wants it.
